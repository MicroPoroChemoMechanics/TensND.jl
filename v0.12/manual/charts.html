Charts and coordinate conversions ​

See also

Theory: Spheroidal coordinates · Ellipsoidal coordinates · Curvilinear differential calculus · Changes of coordinates · Spheroidal harmonics and their re-expansion

Manual: Coordinate systems (symbolic) · Spheroidal harmonics

Tutorials: The spheroid in spheroidal coordinates · Ellipsoidal coordinates · Changing the focal distance of spheroidal harmonics

How to pass from the coordinates of a point to its Cartesian components and back, and from one coordinate system to another. The formulas are on Changes of coordinates.

A chart ​

A chart holds the parameters of a coordinate system and maps numbers to numbers. The predefined ones:

ChartCoordinatesBuilt with
PolarChartPolarChart()
CylindricalChartCylindricalChart()
SphericalChartSphericalChart()
ProlateSpheroidalChartProlateSpheroidalChart(c)
OblateSpheroidalChartOblateSpheroidalChart(c)
EllipsoidalChartEllipsoidalChart(h₂, h₃) or EllipsoidalChart(a₁, a₂, a₃)

The coordinates are ordered as in the system of the same name (Coordinate systems (symbolic)).

From coordinates to a point and back ​

cartesian_to_coords returns the coordinates of a point, and coords_to_cartesian the point of given coordinates:

julia
using TensND

chart = ProlateSpheroidalChart(1.5)      # semi-focal distance c = 1.5
x = [0.4, -0.3, 1.2]
ξ = cartesian_to_coords(chart, x)        # (ϕ, p, q)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 -0.6435011087932843
  0.7209369513548887
  1.1096670776779087
julia
coords_to_cartesian(chart, ξ)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
  0.39999999999999997
 -0.2999999999999999
  1.2

The coordinate is half the sum of the distances from the point to the foci  , divided by :

julia
using LinearAlgebra
(norm(x - [0, 0, 1.5]) + norm(x + [0, 0, 1.5])) / (2 * 1.5)
1.1096670776779087

From one system to another ​

change_coords composes the two maps. The same point in an oblate chart of semi-focal distance 0.8, then in the spherical chart:

julia
change_coords(OblateSpheroidalChart(0.8), chart, ξ)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 -0.6435011087932843
  0.9430483927553549
  1.59058645507827
julia
change_coords(SphericalChart(), chart, ξ)       # (θ, ϕ, r)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
  0.39479111969976144
 -0.6435011087932843
  1.2999999999999998

The ellipsoidal chart ​

An ellipsoidal chart is defined by its two semi-focal distances  , or by the semi-axes    of one ellipsoid of the family, on which  :

julia
ell = EllipsoidalChart(3.0, 2.0, 1.0)
x = [1.8, 1.2, 0.5]
x[1]^2 / 9 + x[2]^2 / 4 + x[3]^2          # inside the ellipsoid: < 1
0.97
julia
ρ, μ, ν = cartesian_to_coords(ell, x)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 2.9875423595709925
 2.6268380177291273
 1.450624926844253

The chart covers the quarter space  ,  ; a point of another quarter gets the coordinates of its mirror image, keeping the sign of :

julia
coords_to_cartesian(ell, cartesian_to_coords(ell, [-1.8, -1.2, 0.5]))
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 -1.8
  1.2000000000000008
  0.4999999999999937

Derivatives ​

Both maps accept ForwardDiff duals, in the point and in the parameters of the chart. The Jacobian of the inverse map has the gradients of the coordinates as rows, :

julia
using ForwardDiff
J = ForwardDiff.jacobian(x -> cartesian_to_coords(chart, x), [0.4, -0.3, 1.2])
3×3 Matrix{Float64}:
  1.2        1.6       0.0
 -0.180108   0.135081  0.499259
  0.277222  -0.207916  0.156262

and the derivative with respect to the semi-focal distance tells how the coordinates of a fixed point move with the foci:

julia
ForwardDiff.derivative(c -> cartesian_to_coords(ProlateSpheroidalChart(c), [0.4, -0.3, 1.2]), 1.5)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
  0.0
 -0.3243625510509479
 -0.2405187983146102

Symbols ​

The direct map has no branch and accepts symbols; it returns the position vector of the system:

julia
using SymPyPythonCall
c, ϕ, p, q = symbols("c ϕ p q", real = true)
coords_to_cartesian(ProlateSpheroidalChart(c), (ϕ, p, q))

The inverse maps select a root or a sign from values: they take numbers.

A pointwise system from a chart ​

The spheroidal and ellipsoidal systems exist in pointwise form, with the operators of Numerical coordinate systems:

julia
CS = coorsys_ellipsoidal(PointwiseGeometry, 2.0, 1.0)     # h₂ = 2, h₃ = 1
lame_coefficients(CS, [2.5, 1.5, 0.4])
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.436043948569201
 1.9549205318156246
 1.9864449505754411

The harmonics that separate in the spheroidal charts, and their change from one chart to another, are on Spheroidal harmonics.
