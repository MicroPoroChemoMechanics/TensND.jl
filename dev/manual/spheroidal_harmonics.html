Spheroidal harmonics ​

See also

Theory: Spheroidal harmonics and their re-expansion · Spheroidal coordinates

Manual: Charts and coordinate conversions

Tutorials: Changing the focal distance of spheroidal harmonics · The spheroid in spheroidal coordinates

How to evaluate the harmonic functions that separate in a spheroidal chart, and how to express a harmonic of one chart in the harmonics of another chart of the same axis. The definitions and the proofs are on Spheroidal harmonics and their re-expansion.

Legendre functions ​

Every function returns the values for all the degrees up to N at once, the degree n at the index n + 1; the entries of degree lower than the order m are zero.

julia
using TensND

legendre_P(3, 1, 0.5)          # P₀¹, P₁¹, P₂¹, P₃¹ at x = 0.5
4-element Vector{Float64}:
  0.0
 -0.8660254037844386
 -1.299038105676658
 -0.3247595264191645

The radial factors depend on the chart: and in a prolate chart, their real counterparts and in an oblate chart, and in the spherical chart.

julia
chart = ProlateSpheroidalChart(1.0)
radial_P(chart, 3, 1, 2.0)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
  0.0
  1.7320508075688772
 10.392304845413264
 49.363448015713004
julia
radial_Q(chart, 3, 1, 2.0)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
  0.0
 -0.20327438748290555
 -0.06494578651818185
 -0.019817351366550908

radial_Q stays accurate at high degree, where the upward recurrence would lose the function to rounding:

julia
radial_Q(chart, 60, 0, 2.0)[[1, 21, 41, 61]]
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.5493061443340548
 3.934964397059358e-13
 1.0224311128620201e-24
 3.048187174792839e-36

Harmonics at a point ​

interior_harmonics and exterior_harmonics take the coordinates of the point in the chart and return the products   and  . Multiplied by or , they are harmonic.

julia
x = [0.7, -0.4, 2.1]
ξ = cartesian_to_coords(chart, x)              # (ϕ, p, q)
interior_harmonics(chart, 3, 1, ξ)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
    0.0
   -0.806225774829855
  -15.237667144284265
 -146.8439470630728
julia
exterior_harmonics(chart, 3, 1, ξ)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.0
 0.058193388217008375
 0.04422291999062725
 0.020406559962754797

Interior harmonics in another chart ​

An interior harmonic of degree of a chart is a finite sum of the interior harmonics of degrees   of any coaxial chart. interior_reexpansion returns the coefficients, the target chart first:

julia
from, to = ProlateSpheroidalChart(1.0), ProlateSpheroidalChart(1.5)
L = interior_reexpansion(to, from, 4, 0)       # degrees 4, 2, 0
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 5.0625
 7.03125
 1.9921874999999996
julia
ξ₂ = change_coords(to, from, ξ)
interior_harmonics(from, 4, 0, ξ)[5], sum(L .* interior_harmonics(to, 4, 0, ξ₂)[5:-2:1])
(33.21203906250005, 33.21203906250001)

The two charts may be prolate, oblate or spherical in any combination. In the spherical chart the harmonics are the solid harmonics :

julia
interior_reexpansion(SphericalChart(), from, 2, 0)    # in r² P₂(cos θ) and 1
2-element Vector{Float64}:
  1.5
 -0.5

Exterior harmonics in another chart ​

An exterior harmonic of degree is an infinite series of the exterior harmonics of degrees   of the other chart. exterior_reexpansion returns its first imax + 1 coefficients:

julia
K = exterior_reexpansion(to, from, 2, 1, 30)
K[1:4]
4-element Vector{Float64}:
  0.2962962962962963
 -0.22222222222222224
  0.08067150042458686
  0.0120556656336263
julia
H = exterior_harmonics(to, 2 + 2 * 30, 1, ξ₂)[3:2:end]     # degrees 2, 4, …, 62
exact = exterior_harmonics(from, 2, 1, ξ)[3]
[abs(sum(K[1:N+1] .* H[1:N+1]) / exact - 1) for N in (2, 5, 10, 20)]
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.00010400614269001629
 6.21249019783221e-7
 2.3697710460623966e-11
 1.1102230246251565e-16

The series converges beyond the coordinate surface of the target chart that passes through the edge of the focal set of the source chart, which reexpansion_bound returns as a value of the radial coordinate:

julia
reexpansion_bound(to, from), ξ₂[3]       # the point is beyond the bound
(1.0, 1.5647203037504775)
julia
reexpansion_bound(from, to)              # the other way: q₁ must exceed c₂/c₁
1.5

Derivatives and symbols ​

The Legendre functions and the coefficients accept ForwardDiff duals. The functions of the first kind and the coefficients, which involve no iteration, also accept symbols:

julia
using SymPyPythonCall
c₁, c₂ = symbols("c₁ c₂", positive = true)
interior_reexpansion(ProlateSpheroidalChart(c₂), ProlateSpheroidalChart(c₁), 2, 0)

radial_Q sizes an iteration on the value of its argument: it takes numbers.
