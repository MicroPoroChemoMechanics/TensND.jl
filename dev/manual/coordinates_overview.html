Charts, coordinate systems and surfaces ​

See also

The package has three kinds of objects for curvilinear coordinates. They answer different questions, and each of the last two comes in an exact and a pointwise form. This page says which to take; the pages that follow give the details.

to …takebuilt withit holds
go from coordinates to a point, and backa chartSphericalChart(), ToroidalChart(a), …the parameters of the system; two maps between numbers
differentiate a field in the coordinates of the spacea coordinate systemcoorsys_spherical() (exact), coorsys_spherical(PointwiseGeometry) (pointwise)the bases, the Lamé coefficients, the Christoffel symbols and the operators grad, symgrad, divergence, laplacian, hess
work on a surface embedded in the spacea submanifoldSubmanifold(OM, coords) (exact), Submanifold(OM_func, dim) (pointwise)the tangent frame and the normal, the two fundamental forms, the same operators along the surface

A chart: coordinates and points ​

A chart maps the coordinates of a point to its Cartesian components and back. It knows nothing of bases or derivatives, works on numbers and duals, and is what places a point, or locates one in an inclusion (Charts and coordinate conversions):

julia
using TensND
chart = SphericalChart()                       # (θ, φ, r)
x = coords_to_cartesian(chart, (π / 3, π / 4, 2.0))
cartesian_to_coords(chart, x)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 1.0471975511965976
 0.7853981633974482
 2.0

A coordinate system: fields and their derivatives ​

A coordinate system adds the differential geometry. Its exact form works on expressions of the coordinate symbols and gives closed forms (Coordinate systems (symbolic)); its pointwise form takes a field as a function of the coordinates and returns the derivative as a function, evaluated by automatic differentiation (Coordinate systems (numerical)). The operators have the same names in both:

julia
using SymPyPythonCall
Spherical = coorsys_spherical()
θ, φ, r = get_coords(Spherical)
laplacian(r^3, Spherical)                      # exact: an expression

julia
pointwise = coorsys_spherical(PointwiseGeometry)
laplacian(x -> x[3]^3, pointwise)([π / 3, π / 4, 2.0])     # pointwise: a number, 12 r
24.0

A submanifold: a surface in the space ​

A submanifold is parametrized by its own coordinates, one fewer than the space, and completes its tangent frame with the unit normal. It gives the first and the second fundamental forms, hence the curvatures, and the operators along the surface (Submanifolds). The exact form is built from the position vector as an expression, the pointwise one from a function:

julia
R = 2.0
sphere = Submanifold(u -> R * [sin(u[1])cos(u[2]), sin(u[1])sin(u[2]), cos(u[1])], 3)
u = [π / 3, π / 4]
laplacian(v -> cos(v[1]), sphere)(u), -2cos(u[1]) / R^2      # Laplace–Beltrami of cos θ
(-0.25000000000000006, -0.25000000000000006)
