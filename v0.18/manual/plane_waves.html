Two-index components

, , , … are the two-index (Voigt) components of the stiffness,   standing for the pairs 11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12 ( ,  ), not the entries of the Kelvin–Mandel matrix, which carry factors and . Both matrices are written in full in Contrast with Voigt.

A plane wave      of unit direction travels in a homogeneous medium of stiffness and mass density when its phase velocity and its polarization solve the Christoffel problem

Three waves go along each direction: the fastest, quasi-longitudinal (qP), and two quasi-transverse ones (qS1 faster than qS2). This page gives the syntax; the derivations are on Plane waves.

to …write

read the phase velocities onlyphase_velocities(ℂ, ρ, n)
read the slowness vectors slowness(plane_waves(ℂ, ρ, n))

get the characteristic polynomial of christoffel_invariants(ℂ, ρ, n)
read Thomsen's parametersthomsen_parameters(ℂ, ρ), ti_constants(ℂ; form = :thomsen, density = ρ)
build a stiffness from themti_from_constants(V_P0, V_S0, ε, δ, γ, axis; form = :thomsen, density = ρ)
velocities to first order in Thomsen's parametersweak_wave_modes(ℂ, ρ, n), anellipticity(ℂ)

The three waves of a direction ​

plane_waves takes the stiffness, the density and a direction, any nonzero vector of three components, normalized on the way. With the stiffness in GPa and the density in g/cm³, the velocities come out in km/s. The example is a transversely isotropic stiffness of axis , given by its components (Parametrizations and conversions):

julia
using TensND, LinearAlgebra

ℂ = ti_from_constants(52.9, 15.7, 21.8, 47.8, 16.4, [0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
ρ = 2.45
n = [1.0, 0.0, 1.0]
w = plane_waves(ℂ, ρ, n)
PlaneWaves along (0.7071067811865475, 0.0, 0.7071067811865475): V = (4.629872318322026, 2.6726124191242433, 2.4102241130689674)

The result is a PlaneWaves: its fields direction (unit), velocity (qP, qS1, qS2), polarization and group hold one wave per column.

julia
w.polarization

 -0.730276   0.0   0.683152
  0.0       -1.0   0.0
 -0.683152   0.0  -0.730276

phase_velocities returns the field velocity alone, and slowness the vectors  , one per column:

julia
phase_velocities(ℂ, ρ, n) == w.velocity, slowness(w)

Closed forms ​

wave_modes gives the waves of an isotropic or of a transversely isotropic medium in closed form, under the name of their mode: P and S, or qP, qSV (polarized in the plane of the axis and of ) and SH (normal to it). Each mode is a pair (velocity, polarization); no velocity is compared with another, so that a mode keeps its name where two velocities cross.

julia
modes = wave_modes(ℂ, ρ, n)
(qP = (4.629872318322027, (20.41750832571323, 19.1)), qSV = (2.410224113068968, (-17.867508325713217, 19.1)), SH = (2.6726124191242437, [0.0, 1.0, 0.0]))
julia
map(first, modes)
(qP = 4.629872318322027, qSV = 2.410224113068968, SH = 2.6726124191242437)

The polarizations of qP and qSV are given by their components along the transverse direction and along the axis, in that order, for a direction on the side of the axis,   ; the direction carries the same waves. An isotropic medium has one shear velocity for every polarization normal to , and the polarization of S is nothing:

julia
wave_modes(iso_stiffness(20.0, 12.0), 2.5, [0.0, 0.0, 1.0])
(P = (3.794733192202055, (0.0, 0.0, 1.0)), S = (2.1908902300206643, nothing))

No closed form needs a comparison, so the constants may be symbols:

julia
using SymPyPythonCall

C₁₁, C₁₂, C₁₃, C₃₃, C₄₄, ϱ = symbols("C_11 C_12 C_13 C_33 C_44 rho", positive = true)
ϑ = symbols("theta", real = true)
ℂₛ = ti_from_constants(C₁₁, C₁₂, C₁₃, C₃₃, C₄₄, [0, 0, 1])
simplify(wave_modes(ℂₛ, ϱ, [sin(ϑ), 0, cos(ϑ)]).SH[1]^2)

julia
I₁, I₂, I₃ = christoffel_invariants(ℂ, ρ, w.direction)

Thomsen's parameters ​

A transversely isotropic medium is often described by the velocities of its P and S waves along the axis, and , and three dimensionless numbers , , that vanish for an isotropic medium (Thomsen, 1986) (Thomsen's parameters). thomsen_parameters reads them, by their exact definitions:

julia
p = thomsen_parameters(ℂ, ρ)
(V_P0 = 4.417035666782945, V_S0 = 2.58725289661069, ε = 0.053347280334728055, δ = 0.15766329984276323, γ = 0.0670731707317074)

On ℂₛ, the symbolic stiffness of the previous section, the definitions themselves come out:

julia
map(simplify, thomsen_parameters(ℂₛ, ϱ))
(V_P0 = sqrt(C_33)/sqrt(rho), V_S0 = sqrt(C_44)/sqrt(rho), ε = (C_11 - C_33)/(2*C_33), δ = ((C_13 + C_44)^2 - (C_33 - C_44)^2)/(2*C_33*(C_33 - C_44)), γ = (C_11 - C_12 - 2*C_44)/(4*C_44))

They are also the form :thomsen of ti_constants and ti_from_constants, with the density as a keyword; the converse gives the stiffness back:

julia
ℂₜ = ti_from_constants(values(p)..., [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]; form = :thomsen, density = ρ)
ti_constants(ℂ; form = :thomsen, density = ρ) == values(p), ti_constants(ℂₜ) .- ti_constants(ℂ)
(true, (7.105427357601002e-15, 1.7763568394002505e-14, 2.1316282072803006e-14, 7.105427357601002e-15, -3.552713678800501e-15))

fixes   only: the converse keeps   . Without density, the form :thomsen throws an ArgumentError.

The anellipticity     vanishes when the slowness curve of the P wave is an ellipse; for ℂ and for ℂₛ:

julia
anellipticity(ℂ), simplify(anellipticity(ℂₛ))
(-0.07930807415662047, (C_11*C_33 - C_11*C_44 - C_13^2 - 2*C_13*C_44 - C_33*C_44)/(2*(C_13^2 + 2*C_13*C_44 + C_33*C_44)))

weak_wave_modes gives the velocities to first order in , to compare with the exact ones:

julia
weak_wave_modes(ℂ, ρ, n), map(first, wave_modes(ℂ, ρ, n))
((qP = 4.650045981461081, qSV = 2.3905938424985567, SH = 2.6740205242409267), (qP = 4.629872318322027, qSV = 2.410224113068968, SH = 2.6726124191242437))

With Thomsen's parameters as symbols, the stiffness of the form :thomsen gives back the first-order formulas of Thomsen's parameters, at the angle to the axis:

julia
V_P0, V_S0 = symbols("V_P0 V_S0", positive = true)
εₜ, δₜ, γₜ = symbols("epsilon delta gamma", real = true)
ℂᵥ = ti_from_constants(V_P0, V_S0, εₜ, δₜ, γₜ, [0, 0, 1]; form = :thomsen, density = ϱ)
wₜ = weak_wave_modes(ℂᵥ, ϱ, [sin(ϑ), 0, cos(ϑ)])
(qP = V_P0*(delta*(1 - cos(theta)^2/(sin(theta)^2 + cos(theta)^2))*cos(theta)^2/(sin(theta)^2 + cos(theta)^2) + 1 + (1 - cos(theta)^2/(sin(theta)^2 + cos(theta)^2))^2*(V_P0^2*rho*(2*epsilon + 1) - V_P0^2*rho)/(2*V_P0^2*rho)), qSV = V_S0*(V_P0^2*(1 - cos(theta)^2/(sin(theta)^2 + cos(theta)^2))*(-delta + (V_P0^2*rho*(2*epsilon + 1) - V_P0^2*rho)/(2*V_P0^2*rho))*cos(theta)^2/(V_S0^2*(sin(theta)^2 + cos(theta)^2)) + 1), SH = V_S0*(1 + (1 - cos(theta)^2/(sin(theta)^2 + cos(theta)^2))*(V_S0^2*rho*(2*gamma + 1) - V_S0^2*rho)/(2*V_S0^2*rho)))

[N(subs(v, at...)) for v in wₜ] .- collect(weak_wave_modes(ℂ, ρ, n))

squared_moveout_velocities gives the squares of the short-spread moveout velocities of a horizontal reflector in a medium of vertical axis, exact for any strength of the anisotropy. The squares are returned because the qSV one is negative in many rocks, where no moveout velocity exists:

julia
squared_moveout_velocities(ℂ, ρ)
(qP = 25.662290393864556, qSV = 2.6234238918497264, SH = 7.591836734693875)

In Thomsen's parameters:

julia
map(simplify, squared_moveout_velocities(ℂᵥ, ϱ))
(qP = V_P0^2*(2*delta + 1), qSV = -2*V_P0^2*delta + 2*V_P0^2*epsilon + V_S0^2, SH = V_S0^2*(2*gamma + 1))

Any symmetry ​

The stiffness of a rock without any symmetry, the Lavoux limestone (Mensch and Rasolofosaon, 1997), is read from its matrix in the two-index notation (stiffness_from_voigt), in GPa, and its density in kg/m³:

julia
using JSON

data = JSON.parsefile(joinpath(pkgdir(TensND), "data", "literature", "menschGJI1997.json"))
rows = data["tables"]["lavoux_stiffness"]
ℂₗ = stiffness_from_voigt(Float64[row[c] for row in rows, c in ("1", "2", "3", "4", "5", "6")])
ρₗ = data["quantities"]["lavoux_density"]["value"] / 1000       # g/cm³

weak_plane_waves gives the three waves to first order in the deviation of the stiffness from isotropy, each as a pair (velocity, polarization), without any reference medium (Weak anisotropy of any symmetry):

julia
nₗ = [0.3, -0.5, 0.8]
map(first, weak_plane_waves(ℂₗ, ρₗ, nₗ)), phase_velocities(ℂₗ, ρₗ, nₗ)
((qP = 3.3834693939661333, qS1 = 1.6637568911233438, qS2 = 1.6357761025518611), [3.3848654176609725, 1.6635528115622042, 1.633093303461875])

generalized_thomsen_parameters returns the seventeen dimensionless parameters that set these velocities about the vertical (Mensch and Rasolofosaon, 1997):

julia
map(x -> round(x; digits = 4), generalized_thomsen_parameters(ℂₗ))
(εx = -0.0566, εy = -0.0453, δx = -0.0792, δy = -0.0981, δz = -0.1434, χx = -0.0415, χy = 0.0151, χz = -0.0075, ε15 = 0.0226, ε16 = -0.0189, ε24 = -0.0075, ε26 = 0.0415, ε34 = 0.0038, ε35 = -0.0151, γx = 0.0673, γy = -0.0339, γz = -0.0593)

For a transversely isotropic medium of axis they reduce to Thomsen's:    and   exactly,   equal to to first order.

julia
g = generalized_thomsen_parameters(ℂ)
(g.εx, g.εy, p.ε), (g.γy, p.γ), (g.δx, g.δy, p.δ)
((0.053347280334728055, 0.053347280334728055, 0.053347280334728055), (0.0670731707317074, 0.0670731707317074), (0.14225941422594138, 0.14225941422594138, 0.15766329984276323))

For an orthotropic stiffness in its axes, given by its nine components , the parameters are ratios of these components, and those that couple the axes vanish:

julia

wave_modes, christoffel_invariants, thomsen_parameters, anellipticity, weak_wave_modes, squared_moveout_velocities, weak_plane_waves and generalized_thomsen_parameters also take symbols, as the examples above show. plane_waves and shear_splitting sort the waves by their velocities, a comparison that symbols do not allow: they throw an ArgumentError on a symbolic stiffness.
