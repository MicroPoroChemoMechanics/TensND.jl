Two-index components

, , , … are the two-index (Voigt) components of the stiffness,   standing for the pairs 11, 22, 33, 23, 13, 12 ( ,  ), not the entries of the Kelvin–Mandel matrix, which carry factors and . Both matrices are written in full in Contrast with Voigt.

A wave      meets a plane interface of unit normal . All the waves it excites share the tangential part of its slowness (Snell–Descartes), and in each medium the normal slowness of     solves a Christoffel problem with six roots, real or in conjugate pairs (Waves at an interface). This page gives the syntax.

The six waves of a medium ​

The two media of the examples are isotropic, built from their P and S velocities (km/s) and their densities (g/cm³), so that slownesses are in s/km:

julia
using TensND, LinearAlgebra

iso(α, β, ρ) = iso_stiffness(ρ * (α^2 - 4β^2 / 3), ρ * β^2)       # bulk and shear moduli, GPa
ℂ₁, ρ₁ = iso(2.9, 1.4, 2.3), 2.3
ℂ₂, ρ₂ = iso(3.5, 2.1, 2.4), 2.4
ν = [0.0, 0.0, 1.0]
p = sind(30) / 2.9                     # tangential slowness of a P wave at 30^\circ in the medium 1
forward, backward = interface_waves(ℂ₁, ρ₁, ν, [p, 0.0, 0.0])

interface_waves returns two tuples of three InterfaceWave: the forward waves carry energy, or decay, towards , the backward ones towards ; each tuple is sorted by increasing , the quasi-longitudinal wave first. The part of sₜ along is removed. Each wave has the fields normal_slowness (, complex), slowness, polarization (unit, complex), traction (    ), flux (the energy flux through the interface per unit ) and homogeneous:

julia
[(w.normal_slowness, w.flux, w.homogeneous) for w in forward]
3-element Vector{Tuple{ComplexF64, Float64, Bool}}:
 (0.2986294495808411 + 0.0im, 5.776389443242207, 1)
 (0.6931648906143003 + 0.0im, 3.1247873268892614, 1)
 (0.6931648906143003 + 0.0im, 3.1247873268892654, 1)

The two shear waves of an isotropic medium share their normal slowness; their polarizations are then any orthonormal pair of their plane. A real is a homogeneous wave; past its critical angle a wave turns evanescent, with a complex of positive imaginary part for a wave that decays towards . In the medium 2 the P wave is evanescent for a tangential slowness above s/km:

julia
[(w.normal_slowness, w.homogeneous) for w in first(interface_waves(ℂ₂, ρ₂, ν, [0.32, 0.0, 0.0]))]
3-element Vector{Tuple{ComplexF64, Bool}}:
 (2.949029909160572e-17 + 0.14410880243335414im, 0)
 (0.3526434029079694 + 0.0im, 1)
 (0.3526434029079694 + 0.0im, 1)

Reflection and transmission ​

The incident wave is a forward wave of the medium 1, which lies on the side   . reflection_transmission returns the three reflected waves of the medium 1, the three transmitted waves of the medium 2, their complex amplitudes R and T per unit incident amplitude, and energy, the fractions of the incident energy flux carried by each wave, as a pair (reflected, transmitted):

julia
incident = forward[1]
r = reflection_transmission(ℂ₁, ρ₁, ℂ₂, ρ₂, ν, incident)
abs.(r.R), abs.(r.T)
([0.028719831796076505, 0.0, 0.17837643716188928], [0.9019708875947696, 0.21781319830476914, 0.0])
julia
r.energy, sum(r.energy[1]) + sum(r.energy[2])
(([0.0008248287383949272, 0.0, 0.017212302474030574], [0.9433769479309296, 0.038585920856644945, 0.0]), 1.0)

The amplitudes are those of the displacement on unit polarizations, whose sign or phase is arbitrary: their moduli, the displacement   and the energy fractions are meaningful. Two waves of the same normal slowness (the shear waves of an isotropic medium) are read together, by the sum of their displacements or of their energies. An evanescent wave carries no energy across the interface.

The interface above is welded: displacement and traction are continuous. With slip, a   matrix (angular frequency times the compliance of the interface, in the unit of a length per stress), the displacement jumps by times the traction (Schoenberg, 1983; Schoenberg and Sayers, 1995):

julia
Z = Diagonal([2.0e-4, 2.0e-4, 1.0e-4])                  # tangential and normal compliances, km/GPa
rₛ = reflection_transmission(ℂ₁, ρ₁, ℂ₂, ρ₂, ν, incident; slip = 2π * 50.0 * Z)
abs.(rₛ.R), sum(rₛ.energy[1]) + sum(rₛ.energy[2])
([0.09186899311356254, 0.0, 0.20301752979892854], 1.0000000000000007)

Both functions take dual numbers, through the derivative of each root: the derivative of a coefficient with respect to the density of the medium 2,

julia
using ForwardDiff

ForwardDiff.derivative(ϱ -> abs(reflection_transmission(ℂ₁, ρ₁, ℂ₂, ϱ, ν, incident).R[1]), ρ₂)
0.08167761968828077

Free surface ​

free_surface_reflection gives the three waves reflected at a free surface, where the traction vanishes, with their amplitudes and energies:

julia
f = free_surface_reflection(ℂ₁, ρ₁, ν, incident)
abs.(f.R), f.energy, sum(f.energy)
([0.7838931469849527, 0.0, 0.844183168059126], [0.6144884658899727, 0.0, 0.3855115341100271], 0.9999999999999998)

Weak contrast ​

weak_contrast_reflection gives the reflection coefficients to first order in the contrast between the medium of the incident wave and the other, from the waves of the first medium only (Waves at an interface). Against the exact coefficients for a contrast of a few per cent:

julia
δℂ, δρ = 0.03 * ℂ₁, 0.02 * ρ₁
w = weak_contrast_reflection(ℂ₁, ρ₁, δℂ, δρ, ν, incident)
abs(w.R[1]), abs(reflection_transmission(ℂ₁, ρ₁, ℂ₁ + δℂ, ρ₁ + δρ, ν, incident).R[1])
(0.00983749504558065, 0.009707975891665244)

Numbers, dual numbers, symbols ​

The functions above take numbers and dual numbers. The six normal slownesses are the roots of a polynomial of degree six, which have no closed form in general: they are found numerically, then sorted and split into forward and backward waves by comparisons of their imaginary parts and of their energy fluxes, which symbols do not allow, and a symbolic stiffness is refused with an ArgumentError. An isotropic stiffness is the exception: its waves have closed forms, and with the symbols of Symbolics (below the critical angles, for a nonzero tangential slowness) interface_waves returns them, and weak_contrast_reflection the first-order coefficients as formulas. The coefficient of the P-P reflection on a contrast of density alone, the moduli unchanged:

julia
using Symbolics
Symbolics.@variables kₛ μₛ ρₛ pₛ δρₛ
ℂₛ = TensISO{3}(3kₛ, 2μₛ)
fₛ, _ = interface_waves(ℂₛ, ρₛ, ν, [pₛ, 0, 0])
Rₛ = weak_contrast_reflection(ℂₛ, ρₛ, 0 * ℂₛ, δρₛ, ν, fₛ[1]).R[1]
Symbolics.simplify(Symbolics.simplify_fractions(real(Rₛ)); expand = true)

The tutorial Reflection and transmission at an interface derives in this way the classical linearized coefficient of a contrast of the three properties.

Characteristic roots of a stiffness of the plane ​

Without inertia, the same problem in the plane gives the displacements    in equilibrium in a medium of stiffness . With the compliances of the plane in the engineering form,    ,    ,    , the characteristic roots solve (Lekhnitskii, 1981)

which has no real root for a positive definite stiffness: two roots of positive imaginary part and their conjugates (Characteristic roots). The example is an orthotropic stiffness of the plane built from its engineering constants in its material frame, here the canonical one (ortho_from_constants):

julia
ℂₚ = ortho_from_constants(40.0, 10.0, 0.25, 5.0, Basis(0.0); role = :stiffness)
a = engineering_compliances(ℂₚ)                 # (a₁₁, a₂₂, a₁₂, a₆₆, a₁₆, a₂₆)
(0.025000000000000005, 0.10000000000000002, -0.006250000000000001, 0.20000000000000007, 0.0, 0.0)

characteristic_pair returns the sum    and the product  , characteristic_roots the two roots:

julia
characteristic_pair(ℂₚ), characteristic_roots(ℂₚ)
((0.0 + 3.3911649915626336im, -2.0 + 0.0im), (0.0 + 2.6309968424748016im, 0.0 + 0.7601681490878319im))
julia
a₁₁, a₂₂, a₁₂, a₆₆, a₁₆, a₂₆ = a
[a₁₁ * μ^4 - 2a₁₆ * μ^3 + (2a₁₂ + a₆₆) * μ^2 - 2a₂₆ * μ + a₂₂ for μ in characteristic_roots(ℂₚ)]
2-element Vector{ComplexF64}:

In the axes of an orthotropic medium   : the quartic is biquadratic,   and    (Characteristic roots). The closed form takes symbols (Symbolics here) for the four components of the stiffness in its axes:

julia
using Symbolics

Symbolics.@variables c₁₁ c₂₂ c₁₂ c₆₆
ℂₛ = TensOrtho(c₁₁, c₂₂, c₁₂, c₆₆, CanonicalBasis{2, Num}())
aₛ = engineering_compliances(ℂₛ)
simplify.(aₛ)
(c₂₂ / (c₁₁*c₂₂ - (c₁₂^2)), c₁₁ / (c₁₁*c₂₂ - (c₁₂^2)), (-c₁₂) / (c₁₁*c₂₂ - (c₁₂^2)), 1 / c₆₆, 0, 0)
julia
sₛ, pₛ = characteristic_pair(ℂₛ)
simplify(real(pₛ) + sqrt(aₛ[2] / aₛ[1])), imag(pₛ), simplify(aₛ[2] / aₛ[1])
(0, 0, c₁₁ / c₂₂)

The product is   . The components of ℂₚ substituted in the symbolic pair give the numeric one:

julia
at = Dict(zip((c₁₁, c₂₂, c₁₂, c₆₆), get_data(ℂₚ)))
number(x) = Symbolics.value(substitute(x, at; fold = Val(true)))
number(real(pₛ)) - real(characteristic_pair(ℂₚ)[2]), number(imag(sₛ)) - imag(characteristic_pair(ℂₚ)[1])

The roots of an isotropic tensor merge at , where they stop being differentiable functions of the stiffness while their sum and product stay smooth. An isotropic, a square or an orthotropic tensor has its pair in closed form, in its own axes turned to the canonical ones; any other tensor has its quartic solved numerically. The same material turned by , as an orthotropic tensor and as a general one:

julia
ℂᵣ = ortho_from_constants(40.0, 10.0, 0.25, 5.0, Basis(π / 6); role = :stiffness)
characteristic_pair(ℂᵣ), characteristic_pair(Tens(Array(canonical_components(ℂᵣ))))
((-0.8294327810893217 + 1.9105154882043016im, -1.1126760563380285 - 0.6618219788434215im), (-0.8294327810893216 + 1.9105154882043012im, -1.1126760563380285 - 0.6618219788434215im))
julia
characteristic_roots(iso_stiffness(Val(2), 5.0, 2.0))
(0.0 + 1.0im, 0.0 + 1.0im)
