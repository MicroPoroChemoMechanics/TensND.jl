Elastic moduli ​

An isotropic linear elastic material has two independent constants, and six of them are in common use: the bulk modulus , Young's modulus , Lamé's first parameter , the shear modulus , Poisson's ratio and the P-wave (oedometric) modulus . Any two of them fix the others. TensND makes the stiffness tensor the meeting point: it is built from any pair and any modulus is read from it, so that a conversion is one function inside another.

The stiffness and its moduli ​

In dimension 3 the stiffness is          , a TensISO{4,3}. The other moduli follow from :

Each has its reader, bulk_modulus, young_modulus, lame_lambda, shear_modulus, poisson_ratio and pwave_modulus, and each pair too, named after its two moduli in the order : bulk_shear, young_poisson, lame_shear, young_pwave, …

julia
using TensND

ℂ = iso_stiffness_young_poisson(70.0, 0.33)   # an aluminum, GPa
(k = bulk_modulus(ℂ), E = young_modulus(ℂ), λ = lame_lambda(ℂ),
 μ = shear_modulus(ℂ), ν = poisson_ratio(ℂ), M = pwave_modulus(ℂ))
(k = 68.62745098039215, E = 70.0, λ = 51.08359133126935, μ = 26.31578947368421, ν = 0.33, M = 103.71517027863777)

A compliance is the inverse of a stiffness, exactly in the class, so its moduli are those of inv(𝕊).

Shorter names ​

The pairs without the P-wave modulus also have the names of their symbols, in ASCII and with Greek letters, as exact aliases: k_mu and k_μ are bulk_shear, E_nu and E_ν are young_poisson, λ_μ and lambda_mu are lame_shear, and the builders likewise, iso_stiffness_E_nu, iso_stiffness_E_ν, iso_stiffness_λ_μ, …. The pairs with have none: k_M or μ_M name the moduli of a matrix in MeanFieldHomogenization, and the two meanings would be read one for the other.

julia
k, μ = k_μ(iso_stiffness_E_ν(70.0, 0.33))
(68.62745098039215, 26.31578947368421)

Symbolic moduli ​

No formula depends on the sign of a value, so the moduli may be symbols. With SymPy, the closed forms come out exactly:

julia
using SymPyPythonCall
λ, μ = symbols("λ μ", positive = true)
ℂ = iso_stiffness_lame_shear(λ, μ)
simplify(young_modulus(ℂ)), simplify(poisson_ratio(ℂ))
(μ*(3*λ + 2*μ)/(λ + μ), λ/(2*(λ + μ)))

They go through ForwardDiff as well: a derivative of a modulus with respect to another pair is that of the composition.

julia
using ForwardDiff
ForwardDiff.derivative(ν -> shear_modulus(iso_stiffness_young_poisson(70.0, ν)), 0.33)
-19.78630787495053

Plane strain and plane stress ​

A 2-D computation on a 3-D material assumes either plane strain,  , or plane stress,  . plane_strain_stiffness and plane_stress_stiffness give the in-plane stiffness of each. Plane strain keeps the in-plane block of ; plane stress eliminates from  , which keeps and replaces by

julia
ℂ = iso_stiffness_young_poisson(70.0, 0.33)
lame_shear(plane_strain_stiffness(ℂ)), lame_shear(plane_stress_stiffness(ℂ))
((51.08359133126936, 26.31578947368421), (25.92301649646505, 26.31578947368421))

Both are TensISO{4,2}, so a plane-stress state is computed with the plane-strain formulas of another material. plane_stress_to_plane_strain gives its moduli and plane_strain_to_plane_stress goes back:

julia
E′, ν′ = plane_stress_to_plane_strain(70.0, 0.33)
young_poisson(plane_stress_stiffness(ℂ)) .≈ (E′, ν′)
(true, true)
