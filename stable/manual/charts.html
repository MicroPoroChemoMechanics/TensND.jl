Charts and coordinate conversions ​

How to pass from the coordinates of a point to its Cartesian components and back, and from one coordinate system to another. The formulas are on Changes of coordinates.

A chart ​

A chart holds the parameters of a coordinate system and maps numbers to numbers. The predefined ones:

ChartCoordinatesBuilt with
PolarChartPolarChart()
CylindricalChartCylindricalChart()
SphericalChartSphericalChart()
ProlateSpheroidalChartProlateSpheroidalChart(c)
OblateSpheroidalChartOblateSpheroidalChart(c)
EllipsoidalChartEllipsoidalChart(h₂, h₃) or EllipsoidalChart(a₁, a₂, a₃)
ToroidalChartToroidalChart(a) or ToroidalChart(R, r)

The coordinates are ordered as in the system of the same name (Coordinate systems (symbolic)).

From coordinates to a point and back ​

cartesian_to_coords returns the coordinates of a point, and coords_to_cartesian the point of given coordinates:

julia
using TensND

chart = ProlateSpheroidalChart(1.5)      # semi-focal distance c = 1.5
x = [0.4, -0.3, 1.2]
ξ = cartesian_to_coords(chart, x)        # (ϕ, p, q)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 -0.6435011087932843
  0.7209369513548887
  1.1096670776779087
julia
coords_to_cartesian(chart, ξ)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
  0.39999999999999997
 -0.2999999999999999
  1.2

The coordinate is half the sum of the distances from the point to the foci  , divided by :

julia
using LinearAlgebra
(norm(x - [0, 0, 1.5]) + norm(x + [0, 0, 1.5])) / (2 * 1.5)
1.1096670776779087

From one system to another ​

change_coords composes the two maps. The same point in an oblate chart of semi-focal distance 0.8, then in the spherical chart:

julia
change_coords(OblateSpheroidalChart(0.8), chart, ξ)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 -0.6435011087932843
  0.9430483927553548
  1.59058645507827
julia
change_coords(SphericalChart(), chart, ξ)       # (θ, ϕ, r)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
  0.39479111969976144
 -0.6435011087932843
  1.2999999999999998

The ellipsoidal chart ​

An ellipsoidal chart is defined by its two semi-focal distances  , or by the semi-axes    of one ellipsoid of the family, on which  :

julia
ell = EllipsoidalChart(3.0, 2.0, 1.0)
x = [1.8, 1.2, 0.5]
x[1]^2 / 9 + x[2]^2 / 4 + x[3]^2          # inside the ellipsoid: < 1
0.97
julia
ρ, μ, ν = cartesian_to_coords(ell, x)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 2.9875423595709925
 2.6268380177291273
 1.450624926844253

The chart covers the quarter space  ,  ; a point of another quarter gets the coordinates of its mirror image, keeping the sign of :

julia
coords_to_cartesian(ell, cartesian_to_coords(ell, [-1.8, -1.2, 0.5]))
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
 -1.8
  1.2000000000000008
  0.4999999999999937

Derivatives ​

Both maps accept ForwardDiff duals, in the point and in the parameters of the chart. The Jacobian of the inverse map has the gradients of the coordinates as rows, :

julia
using ForwardDiff
J = ForwardDiff.jacobian(x -> cartesian_to_coords(chart, x), [0.4, -0.3, 1.2])
3×3 Matrix{Float64}:
  1.2        1.6       0.0
 -0.180108   0.135081  0.499259
  0.277222  -0.207916  0.156262

and the derivative with respect to the semi-focal distance tells how the coordinates of a fixed point move with the foci:

julia
ForwardDiff.derivative(c -> cartesian_to_coords(ProlateSpheroidalChart(c), [0.4, -0.3, 1.2]), 1.5)
3-element StaticArraysCore.SVector{3, Float64} with indices SOneTo(3):
  0.0
 -0.3243625510509479
 -0.2405187983146102

Symbols ​

The direct map has no branch and accepts symbols; it returns the position vector of the system:

julia
using SymPyPythonCall
c, ϕ, p, q = symbols("c ϕ p q", real = true)
coords_to_cartesian(ProlateSpheroidalChart(c), (ϕ, p, q))

The inverse maps select a root or a sign from values: they take numbers.

Unit vectors and Lamé coefficients ​

At a point of given coordinates, unit_vectors returns the unit vectors   as the columns of a matrix of Cartesian components, in the order of the coordinates, and lame_coefficients the coefficients  . Both are closed forms in static arrays, which allocate nothing; on the sphere of radius 2, at the colatitude and the azimuth , the columns are :

julia
ξ = (π / 3, π / 4, 2.0)
unit_vectors(SphericalChart(), ξ)

A tensor that turns with the point ​

A material deposited layer by layer on a sphere, or wound around an axis, has the same tensor of properties at every point in the frame of the unit vectors above, which turns with the point. ChartTens holds such a field: a pattern, whose components in the canonical basis are those of the field in the local frame, and the chart. With the matrix of the unit vectors at the point , the field of a pattern of order 2 is   , and likewise at any order.

A conductivity of value 5 along the radius of a sphere and 1 across it has a transversely isotropic pattern, whose axis is the radial vector of the frame (the third one for the spherical chart, radial_axis):

julia
F = ChartTens(TensTI{2}(1.0, 5.0, radial_axis(SphericalChart())), SphericalChart())
x = [0.3, -0.5, 0.8]
K = tensor_at(F, x)                       # the tensor at the point x, of axis x/‖x‖
n = x / norm(x)
canonical_components(K) ≈ 5 * n * n' + (I - n * n')
true

radial_ti writes the fields that are transversely isotropic about the radial direction, of a sphere or of a cylinder, from the two coefficients of an order-2 tensor or from the five moduli of a stiffness: with the radial direction and , two orthogonal directions across it, Hill's moduli are  ,  ,   ,    and  .

julia
C = radial_ti(CylindricalChart(); n = 12.0, l = 5.0, k = 19.0, m = 11.0, p = 4.0)
eᵣ = Tens([x[1], x[2], 0.0] / hypot(x[1], x[2]))
eᵣ ⋅ (eᵣ ⋅ tensor_at(C, x) ⋅ eᵣ) ⋅ eᵣ      # n, the stiffness along the radius of the cylinder
11.999999999999996

Fields of the same chart add, and a field is multiplied by a number and inverted, as their patterns are. In an exact coordinate system the field is a tensor of constant components in the normalized basis, on which the differential operators act (Coordinate systems):

julia
Sph = coorsys_spherical()
θ, ϕ, r = get_coords(Sph)
@syms kₜ::positive kᵣ::positive s::real
𝐊 = tensor_at(radial_ti(SphericalChart(), kₜ, kᵣ), Sph)
T = r^s * cos(θ)
factor(simplify(divergence(𝐊 ⋅ grad(T, Sph), Sph) / (r^(s - 2) * cos(θ))))

  

The temperature is harmonic for that conductivity when    : the exponent is no longer an integer.

A pointwise system from a chart ​

The spheroidal and ellipsoidal systems exist in pointwise form, with the operators of Numerical coordinate systems:

julia
CS = coorsys_ellipsoidal(PointwiseGeometry, 2.0, 1.0)     # h₂ = 2, h₃ = 1
lame_coefficients(CS, [2.5, 1.5, 0.4])
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 1.436043948569201
 1.9549205318156246
 1.9864449505754411

The harmonics that separate in the spheroidal charts, and their change from one chart to another, are on Spheroidal harmonics.
