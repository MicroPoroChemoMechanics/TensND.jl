Transversely isotropic parametrizations ​

See also

Theory: The Walpole basis

Manual: Structured tensors · Parametrizations and conversions · Symbolic tensors of a material symmetry

A transversely isotropic tensor with major symmetry has five degrees of freedom. Which five numbers one uses is a matter of discipline, not of mathematics, and TensND supports five conventions: the Walpole coefficients, the components, Hill's moduli, the engineering constants and Hoenig's parameters. All of them build a TensTI{4,T,5} and all are interconvertible through the Walpole coefficients of The Walpole basis.

Every matrix below is the Kelvin–Mandel matrix in the frame where  , with the ordering and factors of Kelvin–Mandel representation.

Summary ​

ti_from_constants builds the tensor from five constants and ti_constants reads them back, both according to the keyword form:

formParametersThey are those of
:walpolethe tensor given, stiffness or compliance
:componentsthe tensor given, stiffness or compliance
:hillthe tensor given, stiffness or compliance
:engineeringthe material: its compliance, or its stiffness with role = :stiffness
:hoenigthe material: its compliance, or its stiffness with role = :stiffness

The first three forms are linear in the components: they describe a tensor, whatever it stands for. The last two are constants of a material, which its compliance shows directly and its stiffness through closed forms, never through an inverse. hill_moduli reads Hill's moduli by name, and walpole_coeffs the Walpole coefficients: five with Val(5), those of form = :walpole, or six by default, which also describe a tensor without major symmetry (The Walpole basis).

  • A tensor without major symmetry has no five constants, in any form: it is refused.

Choosing between them ​

Useform
products, inverses and contractions written on the coefficients of the class:walpole
a stiffness known by its components, or symbolic work:components
fiber composites, layered cylinders and spheres, plane-strain problems:hill
material data sheets:engineering
anisotropy ratios, crack and inclusion problems:hoenig

Conversion between any two is a round trip through the tensor itself: build with one form, read with another. Since all five produce the same TensTI{4,T,5}, the conversion is exact rather than fitted — unlike the projection of a general tensor onto the class, which is the subject of Projection onto a symmetry class.
